Open Finance is the Logical Foundation for Agentic Finance

Published on October 14, 2026

Open finance is the logical foundation for Agentic Finance.

The FCA published its PRISM Taskforce report, outlining a framework for assessing which open finance use cases to prioritise. It notes that open finance could create “the technical and trust foundations that AI tools and autonomous agents need to deliver financial services at scale.”

I agree: consent, standards, governance, and accountability are exactly what agents need.

But only a handful of markets have built that foundation. The UK, Brazil, Australia, and India are furthest ahead. The EU's open finance rules aren't in force yet, and in the US the open banking rule has faced court challenges.

Agents won't wait. AI tools already pull account and transaction data to analyse spending, assess credit, and spot risk. Where the regulated route doesn't exist, they'll use whatever access does: screen scraping, private APIs, uploaded statements.

The report scores every use case on the risk of user harm. Add an autonomous agent and those risks sharpen significantly:

  • Consent: How do you revoke access from an agent that has already acted on your data?
  • Nudging: An agent that optimises your finances can also steer them.
  • Fraud and financial crime: Checks have to run at machine speed, in real time.
  • Accountability: When an agent acts, who is liable: the user, the agent provider, or the bank?

Outside the regulated rails, none of these questions even gets asked.

When autonomous agents multiply the speed and volume of activity across financial accounts, real-time fraud and AML monitoring with explainable decisioning is no longer a nice-to-have — it is critical infrastructure.
