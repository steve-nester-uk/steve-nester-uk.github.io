The EUDI Wallet: Changing the Foundation of Identity, AML, and Fraud

Published on November 9, 2026

By the end of 2027, banks and other regulated entities across the EU must accept the European Digital Identity (EUDI) Wallet for strong user authentication under eIDAS 2.0. Member states must make the wallets available by the end of 2026.

This is not just another login method, it changes the foundation of how identity is established and reused:

  • KYC/CDD can shift from repeated document uploads to selective disclosure of government-attested credentials
  • Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) becomes wallet-native instead of SMS or app-based OTPs
  • Account recovery and step-up authentication get higher assurance with less friction
  • Fraud and ATO patterns change — attackers lose easy vectors like SIM swaps and credential stuffing, but new risks appear around wallet compromise and presentation attacks
  • AML transaction monitoring and customer risk scoring will need to incorporate reusable, cryptographically verifiable identity signals

The downstream impact will hit every system that currently treats identity as a one-time or siloed event: onboarding platforms, fraud engines, case management tools, and transaction monitoring systems.

Firms that treat this as a pure authentication project could struggle. The ones that redesign their identity, AML and fraud architecture around portable, high-assurance credentials will gain both compliance readiness and a real operational edge!
