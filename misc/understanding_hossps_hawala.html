Understanding HOSSPs, Hawala, and Professional Money Laundering

Published on September 12, 2026

I’ll be honest: when I first read about HOSSPs I had absolutely no idea what it meant. So I did some research and was fascinated to learn more about Hawala and how professional money launderers (PMLs) are using them.

FATF published a report entitled “Investigating Professional Money Laundering, Underground Banking, and the Use of Hawala and Other Similar Service Providers”. That title caught my attention, so I read the full report.

For those who are unfamiliar, Hawala is an informal mechanism for international funds transfer without money physically moving across borders. It can be used for both legitimate and illegitimate purposes, but often runs outside regulated oversight.

It works by a local broker receiving funds and an international counterpart delivering funds to a recipient. The two brokers then clear through non-standard means such as trade, property, ‘cash pooling’, or other mechanisms — nowadays even digital rails. The key is that there is no direct transfer of funds across borders, making the transaction far harder to detect.

The report highlights current challenges and provides some best practices for disrupting PMLs that everyone in the industry should consider. One notable takeaway:

“Shifting from transaction-based enforcement to system-wide disruption of PML chains.”

This is work that is starting to happen at smaller scale, but the report makes clear it needs to happen globally. To achieve this, stronger international co-operation and mechanisms to coordinate and share data across borders and schemes are essential.

Much like AML in payment orchestration, where transactions are fragmented across multiple rails, data sharing across networks is the clear direction of travel — but has yet to fully materialize.
